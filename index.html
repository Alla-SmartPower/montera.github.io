About

What is Montera?

Montera is an evolving research and engineering platform for automated penetration testing of complex digital infrastructure.

It combines structured pentesting workflows, evidence collection and validation, adaptive decision-making, and research into the reliability of automated penetration-testing results.

The project originated from research in telecommunications cybersecurity and automated penetration testing and has since evolved toward a broader platform applicable to modern IT and telecommunication environments.

Its goal is not only to automate penetration-testing activities, but also to improve the reliability, explainability, and adaptability of the results produced during automated pentesting.

Why it matters

Why Reliability Matters

As penetration testing becomes increasingly automated, the challenge is no longer only whether a system can discover and validate vulnerabilities, but whether the results it produces are sufficiently reliable to support cybersecurity decisions.

Automated pentesting systems may generate false positives, incomplete evidence, incorrect conclusions, or inappropriate prioritization.

In complex and critical infrastructures, including telecommunication networks, the reliability of these results becomes particularly important.

Montera explores how automated penetration testing can become more adaptive, evidence-driven, explainable, and reliable.

Research context

Research Context

Current research explores methods for evaluating and increasing the reliability of automated penetration-testing results and adaptive control of automated pentesting processes using machine-learning methods.

The broader objective is to improve confidence in automated cybersecurity decisions for complex telecommunication and digital infrastructures.

The research focuses on the problem that automated penetration-testing results cannot be considered inherently reliable without evaluating the quality, consistency, and strength of the underlying evidence.

This challenge becomes especially important in complex telecommunication environments, where incorrect automated conclusions may influence further technical and security decisions.

Open questions

Key research questions

Questions that guide the research agenda — without fabricated answers or unverified claims.

  1. How can the reliability of automated penetration-testing results be measured?

  2. How can evidence from different pentesting stages be combined into a meaningful confidence estimate?

  3. How should an automated pentesting system adapt when evidence is incomplete, contradictory, or uncertain?

  4. How can machine-learning methods support adaptive control without reducing transparency and explainability?

  5. How can these methods be applied to complex telecommunication infrastructures?

Platform principles

Design principles

Evidence-Driven

Pentest results should be supported by traceable technical evidence.

Adaptive

Pentesting strategies should evolve based on the current state of the target environment and accumulated findings.

Reliable

Automated conclusions should include mechanisms for evaluating confidence and uncertainty.

Controlled

Potentially intrusive actions must remain governed by explicit scope, authorization, and policy constraints.

Explainable

The system should preserve the connection between technical observations, findings, evidence, and final conclusions.

Research-Oriented

New methods can be experimentally validated within a practical automated pentesting environment.
